# Acceptance criteria: J1 and J5

Given/When/Then scenarios for two of the five journeys in `docs/journeys.md`,
written against the Arcron console on `main` at `69fdbab`.

**How to read the `# Today:` lines.** Each one records what actually happens
now, with file and line. A scenario with no `# Today:` line passes today. A
scenario with one does not, or passes only partly. Everything asserted below
was checked against the code, and the behavioural claims (what `entryFrom`
resolves to on a cold arrival, what `noticesFor` returns for each app id) were
executed rather than read.

**What "the console" means here.** Not the two-tab dashboard that exists, but
the shape `docs/journeys.md` sets out: one browsable surface, search first,
"mine" as a filter, a page per upkeep, no mode to pick. Several scenarios below
are written against that shape and name where today's structure blocks them.

## What was verified, and what it changed

Three things in the brief were checkable, and checking them moved two of them.

**`DEFAULT_NETWORK` is `localnet`, and the front door is worse than described.**
Confirmed at `js/src/networks.ts:64`. Executed: `entryFrom('', null, () => null)`
returns `{"network":"localnet","appId":null}`. The service then builds an
`Algodv2` against `http://localhost:4001` (`js/src/networks.ts:47`,
`web/src/app/core/arcron.service.ts:128-131`), which any browser blocks as mixed
content on an HTTPS host. There is no override anywhere: `web/angular.json`
declares no `fileReplacements` and no `define`, and the one runtime hook,
`window.__ARCRON__`, carries `walletConnectProjectId` and nothing else
(`web/src/app/core/wallets.ts:26-36`).

Two things the journeys document does not say, both of which matter:

1. **`scripts/network.py:53-55` defaults to TestNet.** `js/src/networks.ts:1-8`
   opens with "mirroring `scripts/network.py`" and then does not mirror it. The
   Python half of this project already defaults to the public chain. Only the
   browser half does not.
2. **The URL is rewritten to advertise LocalNet.** `arcron.service.ts:160-163`
   replaces the address on every load with `entryLink(...)`. Executed: a cold
   arrival at a bare hosted URL becomes `/arcron/console/?network=localnet&app=none`.
   So the first link a stranger can copy and send is one that pins every
   recipient to LocalNet, and the property `entry.ts` was written to guarantee
   (a link shows the same registry to everyone) is currently guaranteeing the
   wrong registry.

There is also a hosted build target that nothing runs. `fledge.toml:44` defines
`web-build-hosted` with `--base-href /arcron/console/`. It appears in no lane
(`fledge.toml:63-73`) and in no workflow (`.github/workflows/` has `ci.yml` and
`keeper-bot.yml` only). The address exists in a task definition and nowhere
else.

**`explorerApp` is dead, and two more are missing.** Grep across `web/src` and
`js/src` returns exactly two hits, both inside its own definition
(`js/src/networks.ts:25` and `:59`). There is no `explorerAccount` and no
`explorerTx`, so J1's third clause ("the app, the app account, and any
execution") needs two builders that do not exist, not one that is unused.

**Nothing shows the build hash.** Grep for `sha256`, `hash` and `verify` across
`web/src` and `js/src` returns nothing at all. Stronger than "not displayed":
the console never reads the programs. `refreshApp` fetches
`getApplicationByID` and touches only `params.globalState`
(`arcron.service.ts:236-243`), so `params.approvalProgram`,
`params.clearStateProgram` and `params.creator` are all in the response and all
discarded.

**And the silence is narrower than it looks.** Executed:
`noticesFor({appId: 769891898, network: 'testnet', status: 'ready', frozen: true})`
returns `[]`. But with `frozen: false` it returns the freeze warning, and
`docs/status.md:26` records the live deployment as not frozen. So on the app
that actually exists the console is not silent: it shows exactly one warning
and no identity statement. A stranger on the real TestNet deployment today sees
a warning about the creator's power and nothing telling them they are in the
right place. That is arguably worse than silence, and it is the current state,
not a future one.

## On silence when the app id is the published one

The brief asks whether saying nothing is the right answer for a journey about
verification. It is not, for three reasons the code makes concrete.

**Absence is not evidence, and here it is not even reliable absence.** Executed:
`noticesFor` returns `[]` for the canonical frozen app, for `appId: null`, and
for `appId: null` with a failed read. Three states with completely different
trust meanings, one identical rendering. A reader cannot distinguish "checked
and correct" from "nothing was checked".

**The check that produces the silence is weak.** `trust-banner.ts:29` compares
one integer against `NETWORKS[network].defaultAppId`. It says nothing about the
programs, the creator, or the freeze state. Letting that single comparison
stand in, silently, for "this is the real Arcron" is a stronger claim than the
comparison supports, and J5's own criterion asks for the hash precisely because
the app id alone is not enough.

**The silence is not what a user actually sees today.** The live deployment is
not frozen (`docs/status.md:26`), so the canonical app currently renders exactly
one thing: a warning about the creator's power over their money, with no
statement that they are in the right place. Someone arriving at the real Arcron
sees a page whose only trust output is alarming. That is the opposite of what
the design intended and it is the current behaviour, not a hypothetical.

What I would do: keep the banner exception-only, because a banner that is
usually reassuring is a banner people stop reading, and move identity into a
persistent verification strip that always states the app id, its publication
status, its freeze state, the hash comparison and its date. Then silence in the
banner means "no exceptions", which is true and readable, and the positive
claims live somewhere they can be checked rather than somewhere they can be
skimmed. That is Decision 3, and it is genuinely a decision.

## Decisions the maintainer has to make

### 1. Where the browser's default network lives

`scripts/network.py:53-55` defaults to TestNet. `js/src/networks.ts:64` defaults
to LocalNet under a docstring that says it mirrors the Python file. One of two
things has to give.

**Either** flip `DEFAULT_NETWORK` to `testnet` and make LocalNet reachable only
by an explicit `?network=localnet`, which is a one-line change, brings the two
halves into agreement, and makes the bare hosted URL a live registry. The cost
is the daily development loop: every `ng serve` then opens on TestNet, and a
developer working against LocalNet types a query parameter or relies on the
memory in `entry.ts:56-79`, which is the mechanism the same file argues should
never decide what a stranger sees.

**Or** keep `js/` defaulting to LocalNet and introduce a hosted entry point that
only `web-build-hosted` sets. That keeps both audiences honest, at the cost of a
second configuration axis between local and hosted builds. `web/production-build.test.ts`
exists because exactly that axis has already shipped a 567 KB test harness to
production once, and it can only be defended by a test that asserts against
configuration rather than against output.

The trade is convenience for one developer against correctness for every
stranger, and the second option buys back the convenience by adding the class of
bug the repo has already been bitten by.

### 2. What "the money involved is real" can mean before MainNet

J1 requires a stranger to see that the money is real. On TestNet it is not, and
no amount of wording changes that.

**Either** hold J1 as achievable now and rewrite the criterion to something
TestNet can satisfy: the mechanism is real, the executions are real, the
schedule is real, and the ALGO is explicitly labelled TestNet ALGO with no
monetary value. The console then tells the truth and a stranger's honest
conclusion is that nothing is at stake, which is the exact belief J1 exists to
prevent.

**Or** accept that J1 cannot complete before MainNet and say so in
`docs/journeys.md`, which means the console, already decided as the primary way
people use Arcron, has no front door until a gate that `CLAUDE.md` says any
struct change restarts.

The first option ships a believable page that undercuts its own claim. The
second keeps the claim intact and leaves the primary interface without an
entrance for an unbounded period.

### 3. Where positive identity lives

**Either** the trust banner gains a positive, low-tone notice on the canonical
app, which is a small change confined to `noticesFor`. The cost is that the
banner region becomes usually-reassuring, and a warning region that is usually
green is a warning region people stop reading, which is the failure mode the
banner was built to avoid.

**Or** identity, freeze state, the hash comparison and the app account move into
a persistent verification strip, and the banner stays exception-only. That costs
screen space above the fold on a page where J1 already demands the first screen
explain the product before it asks for anything, and it means designing a second
piece of chrome for a page that currently has one.

This one hinges on whether the front door's scarce first screen belongs to
explanation (J1) or to verification (J5), and they are competing for the same
space.

### 4. Where the expected build hash comes from

**Either** the console hashes the on-chain programs in the browser with WebCrypto
and compares against a constant compiled into the bundle from
`docs/releases.md`. That proves "what is on chain right now equals what this
build expected", which is the claim that matters, and it needs no new pipeline.
The expected value still comes from us, so a compromised bundle can lie about
both sides of the comparison, and the page would be asserting its own integrity.

**Or** the console reads a committed artefact from `scripts/testnet_snapshot.py`,
which records the app, the round and the hash as reproducible evidence, matching
the pattern the public site already uses. That can only ever say "true when we
last checked", and it needs the `snapshot` task actually run somewhere:
`fledge.toml:34` defines it and no lane runs it, so a snapshot that is not
regenerated ages silently into a false claim on a page that looks authoritative.

Live comparison against a hash we control, or recorded evidence that goes stale
without saying so. Doing both is possible and doubles the surface that has to
stay true.

### 5. Whether a trust warning should disable writes

Today no warning disables anything, and the write guards key on
`status() === 'ready'`, which is true on a hostile app and true on a wrong-chain
node.

**Either** make the guard `status === 'ready' && genesisMatches !== false && no
bad-toned notice`. That is one predicate and it closes both holes. It also
breaks self-hosting: every LocalNet app id permanently carries the "No published
app is recorded" notice (verified against `noticesFor` with
`network: 'localnet'`), and any self-hoster on TestNet with their own app id
carries a `bad`-toned notice for as long as they use it. Gating on tone alone
turns a legitimate deployment into a read-only page.

**Or** keep warnings advisory and require an explicit per-notice acknowledgement
before the first write in a session. That preserves self-hosting and puts the
risk at the moment money is committed, at the cost of a click in every honest
session, and acknowledgement dialogs are the control users learn to dismiss
fastest.

The wrong-chain case may deserve to be split out either way, since no legitimate
workflow involves signing against a node that reports a genesis id its
configuration does not list.

### 6. Whether shared links should pin the app id

`arcron.service.ts:160-163` pins network and app on every load, so every link
anyone shares carries a hard app id. `docs/releases.md:141` shows the canonical
app already changed once, on 2026-08-24, and `docs/status.md` names three
retired ids. Every link shared before a redeployment now shows the look-alike
phishing warning for our own former deployment.

**Either** keep pinning and add a superseded tier to `noticesFor`, so an old
link degrades to "this was the published app until <date>, the current one is
<id>" rather than to a phishing warning. That is honest about what the link
actually points at and preserves the property `entry.ts` is built on, at the cost
of carrying a list of retired app ids in the shipped bundle for as long as the
project exists.

**Or** stop pinning the app id when it equals the canonical one, so a bare link
tracks whatever is canonical and only a deliberately-chosen app stays pinned.
Links then heal themselves across redeployments, at the cost of a link silently
resolving to a different contract than it did when it was sent, which is exactly
the property the trust banner exists to defend.

`CLAUDE.md` says a struct change means a new app id and that the surface is
frozen after 1.0, which bounds how often this happens but does not settle it,
because it has already happened and those links exist now.
