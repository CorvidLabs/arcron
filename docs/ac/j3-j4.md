# J3 and J4 as acceptance criteria, and the shape they imply

Scope: `docs/journeys.md` J3 (a creator manages what they have) and J4 (a keeper
earns their first fee), converted to Given/When/Then, grounded in the code on
`main`. Nothing was edited. Line references are to the files as they stand.

---

## 0. The facts I was asked to verify

**`creator` decodes, so "mine" is buildable now.** Confirmed.
`js/src/upkeep.ts:38` declares it and `js/src/upkeep.ts:133` reads it off the
first 32 bytes of the box head. No contract change, no decoder change, no
indexer. The console already computes ownership per row at
`web/src/app/components/registry-table.ts:290` and renders a `yours` badge at
`registry-table.ts:88-90`. So it is not true that the console has *no* concept
of mine; it has the concept and never lets you act on it. There is no filter,
no count, and no way to see ownership at all without connecting a wallet,
because `yours` is derived from `wallet.activeAddress()` alone.

**`board.ts` is most of an upkeep page.** Confirmed. `toEntry`
(`js/src/board.ts:69-90`) gives availability, overdue rounds, net reward,
current effective fee, whether it escalated, runs remaining, and last execution
round. What an upkeep page additionally needs, none of which exists anywhere in
`js/src` or `web/src`:

1. **Does the target app still exist.** Nothing ever calls
   `getApplicationByID` on a target; `arcron.service.ts:236` fetches only the
   keeper app. A deleted target leaves the upkeep classified `due` and listed
   as claimable.
2. **An "unserviced" predicate.** The rule exists, in Python only:
   `scripts/notifier.py:41` (`STALL_INTERVALS = 3`) and
   `scripts/notifier.py:97-102` define stalled as funded, due, and more than
   three of its own intervals past due.
3. **Per-upkeep payout.** `paidToKeepers` is a registry-wide reduce
   (`js/src/board.ts:119-122`), rendered as one tile
   (`upkeep-board.ts:62`). The per-upkeep figure is the same one term.
4. **A cancel refund preview.** The contract refunds
   `balance + BOX_MBR_FIXED + 400 * len(encoded call_args)`
   (`smart_contracts/keeper/contract.py:336-340`); `boxMbr`
   (`js/src/upkeep.ts:88-90`) already computes the second half.
5. **Absolute rounds as wall-clock.** `format.ts` converts *durations*
   (`roundsAsTime`, `format.ts:70-74`) but nothing turns "round 66,685,016"
   into "about 4 hours ago".
6. **Explorer links.** `explorerApp` is defined at `js/src/networks.ts:59` and
   referenced nowhere under `web/src`. There is no equivalent for an account or
   a transaction.
7. **A route.** `web/src/app/app.config.ts:3-5` provides no `provideRouter`.
   The app has no routing at all.

Also: `upkeep-board.ts` declares `lastRun` (`:22`), computes it (`:263-266`),
and never renders it. The board already had "when did this last run" and
dropped it on the floor.

**The Top up wording.** `registry-table.ts:128-135` puts a Top up button on
every row, enabled purely on `canFund`, which is `canSign`
(`registry-table.ts:317`), so a connected wallet can fund any stranger's upkeep
from a table row. The drawer text is `registry-table.ts:166-178`, and the
sentence in question is `:167`:

> Anyone can top up an upkeep, not only its creator. Registered by {{ row.creator }}.

That frames a one-way transfer to a stranger as a feature and never says where
the money can end up. `cancel` pays `Txn.sender` and is creator-only
(`contract.py:297`, `contract.py:342`), so every microALGO you add is
immediately the creator's to withdraw. Proposed wording in section 4, D1.

**No history.** `keeper.service.ts:34` holds `activity` in memory and
`keeper.service.ts:119` caps it at 8 entries. A reload empties it. There is no
total of any kind, and no cost is ever subtracted, so nothing in the activity
log is net. See section 4, D2 for whether that belongs here or in the bot.

### One thing I checked that turned out to be false

`docs/journeys.md:143` says of J4: *"The blind-signing case is fixed: a failed
simulation now throws before the wallet opens rather than after."*

It does not. `discoverResources` (`js/src/keeper-txns.ts:294-319`) reads only
`simulateResponse.txnGroups[0]?.unnamedResourcesAccessed`
(`keeper-txns.ts:317`). It never reads `failureMessage` or `failedAt`.
`AtomicTransactionComposer.simulate` does not throw on a failed group either:
it swallows the failure into `methodResults[i].decodeError`, and the probe
discards the method results. The only place `decodeError` is thrown is
`keeper-txns.ts:79`, on the *real* submission, after the wallet has opened.

Verified live against the published TestNet app 769891898: a simulated
`execute` on an upkeep id that does not exist returned normally with

```
failureMessage: "logic eval error: assert failed pc=1037 ... opcodes=box_len; bury 1; assert"
failedAt: [0]
unnamedResourcesAccessed: null
```

and nothing threw. The two asserts a keeper hits most, `Not due` and
`Insufficient funding`, are separately guarded by the button being disabled
(`registry-table.ts:123`, `upkeep-board.ts:88`, `isExecutable` at
`js/src/upkeep.ts:203-208`), so what actually reaches a wallet is the target's
own failure, which is precisely the case simulation was added for. J4's
"never asked to sign an execution that is already known to fail" is currently
unmet, and the doc records it as met.

### Two more that bear on these journeys

- `keeper.service.ts:86` reports an execution as
  `Executed for ${algos(upkeep.feePerExecution)}`, the **base** fee. The
  contract charges the escalated fee (`contract.py:380-398`). The one number
  the console gives a keeper about their earnings is wrong exactly on the runs
  that paid most.
- `KeeperService.optInAsset` (`:52`) and `topUpAsset` (`:62`) are called by no
  component. The register form will happily register an upkeep with an ASA
  bonus (`register-form.ts:67-79`), and then the console has no way to opt the
  app account in to that asset or to fund the bonus escrow. A creator can
  register, from the console, something the console cannot make work.

### Live state of the published registry, for calibration

App 769891898 at round 66,690,801: 5 upkeeps, all 5 due, 0 dormant, 3 total
executions, median lateness 5,785 rounds, worst 21,119 rounds overdue, 4 of the
5 never run, 1 distinct creator. By the notifier's own rule one of them is
already "going unserviced". J3's unhappy path is not hypothetical; it is the
current state of the dogfood deployment, and the console says nothing about it.

## 3. The information architecture these imply

### 3.1 One surface

Three routes, not two tabs:

| Route | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| `/` | The registry. Search box, filter chips, sort, list. The only browsing surface. |
| `/u/:id` | One upkeep. Everything about it, and every action anyone can take on it. |
| `/register` | The register form, reached by one primary button on `/`. |

Persistent chrome above all three, unchanged from today: network bar, trust
banner, signer bar, stat tiles. The register form stops being pinned below the
list (`app.html:38-41`); the activity strip moves beside the search row rather
than below the fold.

You never pick a mode. A keeper and a creator both land on `/`; what differs is
which chip they press and which actions light up on `/u/:id`.

### 3.2 What search covers

Search runs client-side over the box set already in memory. No indexer, which
is the property `js/src/board.ts:4-6` exists to protect. It matches:

- **Upkeep id**, exact. `21` finds upkeep 21.
- **Target app id**, exact. `769891902` finds every upkeep calling pulse.
- **Creator address**, full or by prefix. Pasting an address is how "mine"
  works without a wallet.
- **Selector**, as hex with or without `0x`. `registry-table.ts:295` already
  renders it.
- **Method signature**, resolved to its selector before matching, so typing
  `tick()uint64` finds the upkeeps that call it. `register-form.ts` already
  computes a selector from a signature; that code is reusable.

What it deliberately does **not** cover: free text. There is none on chain.
Upkeeps have no names, no labels, no descriptions. A search box that looks like
full text and silently is not is worse than one whose placeholder reads
"upkeep id, app id, address, or selector". Say the four things.

An address in the box is also a mode of the page, not just a filter: the header
should say whose upkeeps these are and offer an explorer link for that account.

### 3.3 The filters

Combinable chips, each carrying its own count, each reflected in the URL so any
view is a link:

| Chip | Predicate | Why it exists |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Due now** | `availability === 'due'` | This is the keeper board. Default sort: net reward, descending. |
| **Mine** | `creator === connected address` | J3's "one action". Shortcut for typing your own address into search. |
| **Needs funding** | `availability === 'dormant'` | Today's "Stuck" group. A creator's alarm and an honest account of the network's failures. |
| **Unserviced** | funded, due, and more than three of its own intervals past due | The notifier's rule, ported. The condition J3 says a creator must not learn from the chain going quiet. |
| **Target missing** | target app no longer exists | Money escrowed against a call that can never succeed. Nobody can currently see this. |
| **Scheduled** | `availability === 'scheduled'` | "Coming up". |

Sorts stay orthogonal and reuse `SortKey` (`js/src/board.ts:19`), plus one
addition, `next due`, for browsing rather than working. Both filter and sort go
in the URL. Both survive the 2.5 second poll, as the board's sort already does.

Default on arrival: no filter, whole registry, sorted by next due, with counts
on every chip. That way a returning creator sees "Mine 3" immediately without
the page having decided for them, and a stranger sees the network rather than
an empty personal view. See D5.

The keeper board's real contribution is not its layout, it is its refusal to
hide dormant upkeeps. In a merged surface that survives as chips whose counts
are visible in the default view. If "Needs funding 6" and "Unserviced 1" are
never on screen unless you press them, the merge has lost the thing the board
did best.

### 3.4 The upkeep page

`/u/:id`, readable with no wallet, in this order:

1. **Identity.** Id, target app, the exact call: selector plus each argument as
   bytes, with ARC-4 decoding shown where the signature makes it unambiguous.
   Creator address in full. Explorer links on the keeper app, the app account,
   the target app, the creator, and the most recent execution.
2. **Status,** one line, one of: due, scheduled, needs funding, unserviced,
   target missing. Each with its own "what happens next" sentence. This is the
   only place the vocabulary is defined, and the chips and the list rows use
   the same six words.
3. **Schedule.** Cadence in rounds and in time. Next due, as a round and as a
   time. Last serviced, as a round and as a time ago. Catch-up policy in words,
   with what a replay pays.
4. **Money.** Fee per execution. Ceiling if any, with a sentence on what
   escalation does and that the escrow must cover the ceiling once late.
   Current effective fee. Escrow. Runway in runs and in time. At-least-paid-out,
   labelled a floor. Bonus asset, its per-run amount and its escrow, when there
   is one.
5. **Actions**, gated by who is looking, each stating its cost and effect
   before a wallet opens:
   - Execute: anyone, when due and executable.
   - Top up: anyone, with the gift wording of D1 when it is not yours.
   - Cancel: creator only, with the refund preview.
   - Fund the bonus, and opt the app account in to the asset: anyone, when the
     upkeep uses one. Both already have service methods and no UI.
6. **Provenance.** Which app this upkeep lives in, whether that app is the
   published one, whether its creator can still replace its programs. This is
   J5's material, but it belongs on any page where somebody is about to commit
   money, not only on the front door.

Almost all of section 4's arithmetic exists in `board.ts` and `upkeep.ts`
today. The genuinely new work is the seven items listed in section 0.

### 3.5 What each tab contributes to the merge

**Keep from the keeper board:** net-of-cost pay as the headline number, the
grouping by availability, the deliberate visibility of dormant upkeeps, the
sort control, and the honesty of `paidToKeepers` as a floor. These become the
Due now chip's default sort and the shared status vocabulary.

**Keep from the registry table:** the escalated-fee display with the base
underneath (`registry-table.ts:103-113`), which is the clearest thing in the
console, and the per-row actions. The row shrinks to identity, status, cadence,
next due, pay, and one link; everything else moves to `/u/:id`.

**Lose:** the second tab, the fixed sort by id, and the permanently pinned
register form.

**Watch:** `lastRun` is already computed and thrown away
(`upkeep-board.ts:263-266`). It should land on the row, not just the page.

## 4. Decisions for the maintainer

### D1. Topping up a stranger's upkeep: a row button, a page action, or gone

**A.** Keep it on every list row, relabelled as a gift.
**B.** Remove it from rows; keep it on `/u/:id` only, behind wording that names
the creator and says the money becomes theirs.

A keeps the one-click path to unsticking a stalled public good, which is a real
property of a permissionless network and the fastest cure for the "Needs
funding" chip having a count. It also puts an irreversible transfer to a
stranger one click from a table row where the only mention of ownership is a
short address inside a drawer you have to open first
(`registry-table.ts:167-168`). B costs one click and makes the gift deliberate.

Whichever way this goes, the sentence has to change. Today's reads as a
feature announcement. Something closer to:

> This upkeep belongs to `<full address>`. Anything you add becomes theirs:
> only they can cancel it, and cancelling returns the whole remaining escrow to
> them. Add funds only because you want this call to keep happening.

My read: B for the row, the capability preserved on the page, and the wording
above regardless.

### D2. Earnings history: the console, the bot, or neither

**A.** The console keeps a local ledger in `localStorage`, keyed by connected
address and app id: each execution with the fee actually charged, the fee paid
to run it, the net, the round, the transaction id, plus running totals.
**B.** The console shows the current session only and points at the bot, which
already emits `fee_collected`, `base_fee`, `escrow_remaining`, `next_due_round`
and `tx_id` per execution as JSON lines (`scripts/keeper_bot.py:592-606`).

A answers the question J4 says must be answerable, for the person J4 is about,
with no backend. But it is a per-browser truth that will look like an account:
it can never include anything the bot did, it does not follow the keeper to
another device, and clearing site data destroys it, which is worse than never
having claimed to keep it. B is honest and already exists, but it means the
console cannot answer J4's last criterion at all, and the answer becomes "run a
Python script and read its logs", which sits badly against the decision that
the console is the primary way people use Arcron.

Worth naming a third framing before choosing: the console *could* reconstruct
attribution from the chain, since `scripts/notifier.py:284` already fetches the
block for an execution round and reads the sender. It is bounded to
`MAX_ATTRIBUTION_BLOCKS = 24` blocks, about a minute, so it does not reach an
hour without an indexer, and the no-indexer property is one this project has
deliberately protected.

My read: B plus a bounded A. Persist this browser's own executions, label them
exactly that, show session and all-time-in-this-browser totals, and add one
line saying a bot's earnings live in the bot's logs. Do not build something
that looks like a keeper account. This decision also settles the 8-entry cap at
`keeper.service.ts:119`: either it becomes a ledger, or it is a receipt strip
and should say so on screen.

### D3. "Never sign a known failure": fix the client, or retire the claim

It is currently false (section 0). Two ways to make it true:

**A.** Read `failureMessage` and `failedAt` in `discoverResources` and throw a
message built from them. About five lines, in a round trip already being paid
for, and it turns the deleted-target and reverting-target cases into a
pre-wallet refusal.
**B.** Additionally check on every refresh that each distinct target app still
exists, so the listing itself is honest and the keeper never reaches the
button.

They are not exclusive. The real question is B's request budget: one extra
request per distinct target per refresh, on top of a read that is already one
request per box every 2.5 seconds. A is cheap and reactive; B is the only thing
that makes the "Target missing" chip possible.

Either way, `docs/journeys.md:143` needs correcting, because a doc that records
an unfixed thing as fixed is how it stays unfixed.

### D4. Is "mine" the connected wallet, or any address

**A.** Mine means the connected account. Filter and actions agree by
construction.
**B.** Mine means whatever address is in the search box, with the connected
account as a one-press shortcut.

B is strictly more useful and costs nothing, since `creator` is already
decoded: a creator can check on their upkeeps from a phone with no wallet, and
anybody can audit any creator, including us auditing the dogfood deployment. It
does mean "Mine" is the wrong name for the mechanism, and the page must be
careful that a viewed address is not a signing address, or somebody will press
Cancel and be confused when it is disabled.

My read: B, labelled by the creator address with a "Mine" shortcut chip.

### D5. What the default view is when a wallet is connected

**A.** No filter. Whole registry, with "Mine 3" on its chip.
**B.** Auto-filter to Mine when the connected account owns anything.

B optimises the returning creator, which is exactly J3's user, at the cost of
the first visit, and it quietly reintroduces a mode chosen by the wallet rather
than by the person. A relies on the count doing the work.

My read: A. "You never pick a mode" cuts both ways; the page should not pick
one either.

### D6. Real routes, or a query parameter

**A.** `provideRouter` and `/u/:id`. The app currently has no routing at all
(`app.config.ts:3-5`).
**B.** Stay single-page and select an upkeep with `?upkeep=21`, reusing
`entryLink` (`entry.ts:82-86`).

A is what "each upkeep has a page" means: back and forward, deep links, a
distinct document title per upkeep. B is less work and keeps the link
precedence rules of `entry.ts` in one place, but a page that is really a panel
is the thing J3 says is missing. Under A, `entry.ts`'s precedence for network
and app has to survive as query parameters alongside the path, which is the one
piece of care this costs.

### D7. Where the "unserviced" threshold lives

**A.** Port `STALL_INTERVALS = 3` (`scripts/notifier.py:41`) and the predicate
(`scripts/notifier.py:97-102`) into `js/src`, so the console, the notifier and
any SDK consumer agree, the way `effective_fee` is already deliberately
triplicated.
**B.** Let the console pick its own threshold.

A adds one more constant that must stay in step across Python and TypeScript,
which this repo has paid for getting wrong before. B guarantees a creator can
be told "unserviced" by one tool and not the other, which is worse. Take A, and
put it next to the existing mirrored constants so it is found when it changes.

### D8. Where the register form goes

**A.** Its own route, entered from one primary button.
**B.** Stays pinned under the list.

A follows from "you never pick a mode" and gets nine fields off the surface a
stranger lands on, which is J1's problem as much as J3's. B keeps registration
one scroll away, which matters if registering is what most people come to do.
Note that the form is 394 lines and its cross-field validation is the best part
of the console (`docs/journeys.md:85-87`), so moving it must not turn into
rewriting it.

### D9. ASA upkeeps: finish them or hide them

The console can register an upkeep with a bonus asset
(`register-form.ts:67-79`) and then has no way to opt the app account in to
that asset or to fund the bonus escrow. `KeeperService.optInAsset`
(`keeper.service.ts:52`) and `topUpAsset` (`:62`) are called by no component.

**A.** Surface both on `/u/:id`, and show the bonus wherever an upkeep's pay is
shown.
**B.** Hide the two asset fields in the register form until they can be
followed through.

A is a small amount of UI on a capability 1.0 ships deliberately as a
capability rather than a commitment. B is honest today and costs nothing.
Either beats a form that lets somebody create a thing the product cannot then
operate.
