# What 1.0 is

Decided 2026-08-24. This is the scope of the last struct-changing deployment,
what is deliberately out, and what has to happen before mainnet.

The shape of the plan comes from one hard constraint: **the keeper contract's
`Upkeep` struct cannot be changed in place.** Changing it means a new app id
and an empty registry, and every creator must cancel and re-register by hand
because `cancel` is creator-only. So struct changes are batched into one
release and then the surface is frozen.

> [!NOTE]
> **The premise narrowed on 2026-08-25.** When this was written the constraint
> was flatly that the contract could not be upgraded at all. It now ships
> `update` and `freeze`: a deployment starts unfrozen and its creator can
> replace the programs, and `freeze` gives that up permanently. See
> [`../security.md`](../security.md).
>
> The conclusions below stand unchanged, because the part of the premise they
> rest on is the part that did not change. An update replaces code, not the
> shape of boxes that already exist, so a struct change is still a new app id
> and still a manual migration for every creator. Batching #7, #14, #8 and #9
> into one release is still right, and so is freezing the surface after it.
> What the update path buys is a bug fix while the deployment is young, not
> permission to reshape the struct later. Where the plan below calls a change
> expensive, it still is; the reason is the boxes rather than the programs.

## In

| Issue | What | Why it is in 1.0 |
|-------|------|------------------|
| [#7](https://github.com/CorvidLabs/arcron/issues/7) | Per-upkeep catch-up policy | The shipped demos disagreed about the right behaviour after an outage: `rain` (since 2026-08-31 at [CorvidLabs/arcron-rain](https://github.com/CorvidLabs/arcron-rain)) wanted SKIP_AHEAD (replaying a week of draws in one burst is absurd), and a scheduled distribution wants CATCH_UP (skipping silently loses a period's allocations). A protocol constant is wrong for someone whichever way it is set. Rain leaving does not retire the argument: it is why the policy is per-upkeep, and both policies are in use on the live registry today. |
| [#14](https://github.com/CorvidLabs/arcron/issues/14) | Overdue fee escalation | Without it, minimum-fee upkeeps are second-class and their creators cannot fix that except by cancelling and re-registering higher. **The claim that escalation is what makes a small user's upkeep reliable is the reason it shipped, and it has not held up.** It has never bought a keeper on the live registry, and a third party who can make a target refuse can buy the lateness and collect the escalated fee. [`escalation.md`](escalation.md) has the measurement and the decision, which must be taken before `freeze`. |
| [#8](https://github.com/CorvidLabs/arcron/issues/8) | Resource declaration | [#24](https://github.com/CorvidLabs/arcron/issues/24) measured that keeper-supplied references already reach two levels down; the capability exists and only *discovery* is missing. Without it, targets can never pay an arbitrary account, call a DEX, or read a balance. |
| [#9](https://github.com/CorvidLabs/arcron/issues/9) | ASA fee **capability** | Fees denominated in any ASA. CORVID is not being used; the reason to include it now is that this is the last redeployment, and adding it later would mean another one. |

## Out, deliberately

**CORVID is not wired in.** 1.0 supports ASA-denominated fees; it does not
require, prefer or default to any particular asset. Escrow and fees are ALGO
unless a creator chooses otherwise. "No token required" stays true.

**[#15](https://github.com/CorvidLabs/arcron/issues/15) staking and assignment
windows** is out. It changes the core value proposition and needs review before
any code, and 1.0 is meant to be a surface we can freeze and self-review.

**[#22](https://github.com/CorvidLabs/arcron/issues/22) keeper-supplied call
data** is out. It is a decision about what Arcron *is*, not a feature. Note the
tension with #8: declaring which *resources* a call may touch is safe, because
the creator still fixes what is called; letting a keeper supply *data* is a
different thing entirely, and 1.0 does the first and not the second.

## Sequence

1. **Design #8 and #9** to the standard of [`scheduling-and-fees.md`](scheduling-and-fees.md):
   measured constraints, options with costs, open questions. The batching
   decision cannot be made without them.
2. **Review all four designs together.** They share a struct; the field budget,
   the box MBR and the migration are one conversation, not four.
3. **Implement as one change.** Contract and spec first, then both decoders and
   both pinned box vectors in the same commit, then the bot's selection logic,
   then the console, then the e2e cases. `fledge lanes run local` green
   throughout.
4. **Deploy once.** New app id, docs repointed, the old app deprecated with a
   migration note, following the procedure from
   [#3](https://github.com/CorvidLabs/arcron/issues/3), which is written down
   and has been done once.
5. **Dogfood on TestNet** (below), with a keeper and the notifier running.
6. **Self-review against [#12](https://github.com/CorvidLabs/arcron/issues/12)**
   while it soaks.
7. **Go public** when the first impression is a deployment we are not about to
   replace. The licence, security policy and contributor docs land before that
   ([#50](https://github.com/CorvidLabs/arcron/pull/50)); only visibility waits.

## The dogfood

**The live TestNet registry, to be watched by the notifier (not yet running;
G1 in [`mainnet-rollout.md`](mainnet-rollout.md)), with `pulse` as the
heartbeat target for the uptime clock.**

> [!NOTE]
> **Revised 2026-08-31, and this is the second revision.** It was originally a
> recurring treasury distribution; `treasury` was one of four example contracts
> cut on 2026-08-26 (with `deadman`, `embargo` and `watchdog`), because most of
> four independent reviews' findings landed in the examples rather than in the
> keeper contract, and none of them was the product. It then became a recurring
> `rain` draw, on the reasoning below, which was correct at the time and is
> superseded now. Rain moved to <https://github.com/CorvidLabs/arcron-rain> on
> 2026-08-31 and still runs, as upkeep 113 against the hub it redeployed there;
> it is simply no longer the evidence. (Upkeep 91, which drove the hub rain
> abandoned, is a loose end rather than a dogfood — see
> [`../status.md`](../status.md).)

**Why it was a rain draw, and why that argument expired.** Rain was chosen over
a one-shot settlement for three reasons, all of them still true of rain:

- **It exercises the 1.0 feature we are building.** Rain is the demo that
  *needs* SKIP_AHEAD: replaying a week of missed draws in one burst is
  absurd, and only the latest matters. Dogfooding it tests #7 rather than
  working around it.
- **Its failures are legible.** A draw that resolves the wrong winner, or
  fails to resolve at all, shows up as a specific wrong outcome, not as an
  absence nobody notices. `pulse`, run alongside it purely as a heartbeat,
  covers the other failure mode: a counter that stops incrementing is
  unambiguous, which is exactly what makes it the right instrument for the
  uptime clock even though it proves nothing about #7 on its own.
- **It runs forever.** The mainnet gate is *time on TestNet*, and time only
  counts if something is actually running for it. A one-shot settlement fires
  once and proves nothing about sustained operation.

What expired is not any of those points. It is the premise underneath them:
**that gate was written when we were the only user.** One upkeep we ran
ourselves was the strongest evidence available, because it was the only
evidence available.

It is not any more. 28 upkeeps are live, 22 of them registered by accounts
other than the deployer, across seven addresses, spanning both catch-up
policies and cadences from 1,286 to 224,000 rounds. **Every one of those seven
addresses is ours**, including `CEPY52VZRWFL`, which funded itself from the
public dispenser and ran its own keeper and so was recorded here as a stranger
until [#236](https://github.com/CorvidLabs/arcron/pull/236) attributed it. The count of upkeeps registered by somebody who
is not us is zero.

That is a weaker claim than this paragraph originally made, and it is still
enough for what the gate needs. The registry exercises SKIP_AHEAD, CATCH_UP,
starvation, reverting targets and keeper competition every day, with failures
that are legible in exactly the way the second bullet asks for, and it keeps
doing so whether or not any single target is interesting this week. What it is
not is evidence that anybody else wants this. It is a soak test across every
policy and cadence, run by one operator, and that is what the gate should say
it is.

So the dogfood is the registry, `pulse` remains the heartbeat the uptime clock
reads, and rain is named here as the thing that got us to a registry worth
watching rather than as a component of it. Operationally it is the same
low-risk shape it always was: TestNet ALGO, escrow the top-up tooling keeps
near 30 days of runway, and the notifier announcing failures to a channel. If
it breaks, we find out from the thing that is supposed to tell us, which is
itself the test.

## The mainnet gate

No paid audit. Mainnet is gated on:

- the 1.0 contract **unchanged** for a sustained period, since any struct
  change restarts the clock, which is the point of freezing the surface;
- the **live registry serviced continuously** over that period, with the
  notifier's record as evidence. Not one nominated upkeep: the registry, both
  policies and its full spread of cadences. An earlier version of this bullet
  said "with its outside creators", which is not true and is not something the
  gate can wait for: every address on the registry is ours. It gates on
  sustained operation, which we control, and not on adoption, which we do not.
  This was a single dogfood upkeep until 2026-08-31, and gating on one target
  meant an outage in a repository this one does not control could stall a
  MainNet deployment, which is a dependency the gate was never meant to have;
- [#12](https://github.com/CorvidLabs/arcron/issues/12) completed as a rigorous
  self-audit: invariants enumerated and tested, threat model written, known
  limitations stated, every claim in the docs checked against a chain rather
  than against memory.

`SECURITY.md` says plainly that this code is unaudited, and that does not
change by deploying it to mainnet. What changes is that we will have watched it
work, and that the deployment will have been frozen, so nothing can be quietly
altered underneath anyone. [`../releases.md`](../releases.md) makes recording
that decision an rc gate.
